# Critic A, round 4: NEXUS FABRIC film 3, 54 s

Films: NEXUS-FABRIC-film3-54s-en.mp4 (judged in full) and -ja.mp4 (cross-check). Picture, text and rhythm only; the audio was not heard. Method: six EN contact sheets at 2 fps, then SCRIPT.md, then ten boundary strips (30 fps, 12 frames), eight full-size EN stills, two 390-px phone stills, three JA sheets at 1 fps, four JA full-size stills and one JA toggle strip, plus ffmpeg freeze (≥1.2 s), luma-jump and PSNR measurements. The two 12-frame JPGs already in the final folder were not opened, and no other report was read. Sheet times are ±0.5 s; full-size stills are exact.

## Scores (0–10)

**A. Hook: 5.** Within two seconds a grey tee appears with a blue cut arc drawing round the branch base (0.03–0.33 s), and "One wrong curve." rises at 0.3–0.5 s. The headline stops a thumb; the picture does not. The first frame (0.0 s, full-size) is a plain pipe with no type, brand or plate. From 0.5 to 3.5 s only a slow drift moves (PSNR 25.6 dB between 1.0 s and 3.0 s). The script's "wrong curve, gap opens and flashes" is not on screen: no frame-to-frame luma jump above 4 levels occurs in 0–20 s, and the branch floats about 90 px above the pipe top at 1.0 s, which reads as a render fault rather than a gap.

**B. Clarity: 6.** After one silent viewing a viewer can say: it computes flat patterns for pipe joints, checks its own work, states what it cannot confirm, runs offline, and has an AMOEBA layer. "Dimensions in. Flat pattern out." (8–12) and the five-part catalogue (12–20) carry the what. The why is only implied ("One wrong curve.", "how do you know?"); the cost of a wrong cut is never shown.

**C. Visual design: 5.** Disciplined palette: off-white engineering grid, near-black grotesque, blue for cut and data, amber for flags and REVIEW, green for PASS, gold for NOT RELEASED. The wordmark is top-left from about 2 s. Against that: the tee and the parts are flat gradient glyphs, not a premium object; the headline anchor moves between top-left (8–12, 30–46), top-centre (12–20), a right column (20–26), under the object (0–4) and centre (50–54); several frames are nearly empty (28.0 two outlines, 40.0 a numeral alone, 48.5–50 a corner icon); and the disclaimer changes wording at least four times.

**D. Motion: 4.** The vocabulary is mask-rise and fade for type, counters, line draws, a flat-to-3D pairing, one slow pan and one hard cut into the real app (37.0, +8 luma). There is no camera move, depth or light change. About 15.6 s of the EN film (29%) is still in holds of 1.4–2.3 s (5.8–7.9, 8.6–10.2, 23.6–25.9, 30.9–33.0, 35.5–37.0, 38.5–40.0, 43.7–45.4, 48.6–49.9, 52.6–end); about 5 s of the 12-s AMOEBA section is still. All nine transitions overlap old and new elements for 0.1–0.4 s. The luma-measured cuts at 20.1 and 26.1 land three frames after the bar lines the script locks, and the outgoing fades start about 0.1 s before them. The best-timed motion is the check counter, which steps +4 per beat from 20.5 to 23.5 s.

**E. Product presentation: 5.** The checks card (20–26) is the best interface piece: a large numeral, the product's own check identifiers, a tick per verdict, and "not an approval" stated in full. The identifiers are proof, but they read as code to a shop owner. The two real-software moments are not equal. 10.0–12.0 is credible as product but weak as evidence: cropped through the DXF button at 11.0, no typing, no flat pattern in view, two seconds. 37.0–40.0 is convincing as authentic software (a dense real dependency graph with the product's own labels), but the camera moves a few dozen pixels and holds, so the graph is never read.

**F. AMOEBA, 30–42: 6.** A newcomer can follow the picture. Values are cards with connectors (30–33); a change to 158 mm flips nodes amber while the flag count climbs (33–36); "Replay: match." closes it (41). Missing: AMOEBA is never shown as the layer underneath; "obligations" is jargon; the ripple is a sequence of node colour flips, not a signal along the edges; the old job is never marked superseded here; the correction is not made in the real application.

**G. Ending and call to action: 6.** The cleanest frames in the film: the mark stroke-draws (50.0–50.5), then the wordmark, tagline, black pill "Request a demonstration" (51.6), honest line and AMOEBA line. Weak points: the CTA has no destination (no URL, QR or handle), so a phone viewer cannot act on it; the disclaimer is not the locked line (defect 7); the AMOEBA line fades in late, faint at 52.5 and readable from about 53.0.

**H. Phone readability: 4.** At 390 px (stills at 1.0 s and 22.0 s) the hook headline and "It checks its own work." read well. Card rows are borderline at about 5–7 px. Captions are fine print at about 4 px, so the honesty captions, which carry the film's core promise, cannot be read on a phone. Node labels, spec lines and chips are estimated at 4–7 px from the 1080p frames.

**I. Japanese version: 5.** The cut points match the English in the luma test (20.1, 29.5, 37.0, 51.6 s; the DXF cut at 26 s is softer in JA); the scripted lines are followed; the language flip works (toggle at 47.0, labels 47.1–47.5); the real software is shown in Japanese (37–40). Problems: the check card mixes product identifiers with Japanese sentences (21–22 s; saddle_handedness_matches_3d_intersection sits beside Japanese rows); the JA headline at 1.0 s is about 40% wider and heavier than the English; a voice credit, "音声：VOICEVOX Nemo", appears bottom-right in the final seconds (seen at 53.5) with no English equivalent; the question scene holds 3.4 s (4.5–7.9) against 2.2 s in English; the caption at 12–19 s appears to say 各種の代 where the English says bend allowance (the usual term is 曲げ代; a native engineer should confirm).

**Overall: 5.** A disciplined, honest explainer that plays as a slide deck, not a launch film. Its best idea, honesty as a feature, is nearly lost in micro-type.
**The sentence a viewer will remember:** "What it can't confirm, it shows."

## Must fix to reach 9/10, most damaging first

1. **26.0–30.0, the DXF scene is empty.** At 28.0 both panels are blank outlines (dashed and solid); the filename is static from 28.5; the green match line and stamp arrive by 29.0. Why it hurts: the proof of "written, read back, compared" has no content, this is the worst frame in the film, and "the real file streams past; the read-back draws over the pattern" is not on screen. Fix: fill both panels with the real header flat pattern from product output, at least 900 px wide and centred. 26.0–26.6: real DXF lines scroll up at about 6 lines/s in a 600-px panel. 26.6–27.6: a blue read-back stroke draws over the pattern outline (1.0 s, ease-out). 27.6–28.1: a green match tick draws between the dashed (written) and solid (read-back) panels. 28.5–29.0: NOT RELEASED drops in with a 0.15-s overshoot and holds to 30.0 with the pattern visible.

2. **All nine transitions (4, 8, 12, 20, 26, 30, 42, 46, 50).** Old and new elements share one spot for 0.1–0.4 s: text on text at 4, 8, 20, 26, 30, 42 and 46; a shape on the app screenshot at 12; the icon on the wordmark at 50. At 19.9–20.2 the "Header shell with opening" heading and its caption overlap "It checks" and the card caption. Why it hurts: each cut reads as a glitch, and the bar line becomes the midpoint of a smear. Fix: one text zone per frame. Outgoing elements leave by bar minus 0.1 s (fade to zero and rise 12 px in four frames); incoming elements start on the bar at frame 0. At 20.0, the header heading, its caption and the blue ring are gone before 19.9.

3. **Holds of 1.4–2.3 s (EN about 15.6 s, JA about 18.9 s).** Worst: JA 4.5–7.9 (3.4 s), EN 23.6–25.9 (2.3 s, checks card at 28 of 28), EN 5.8–7.9 (2.2 s, loop), EN 30.9–33.0 (2.1 s, AMOEBA cards). Why it hurts: over a quarter of the film is still, the largest single cause of the slide feel. Fix: every hold gets one beat-locked action per bar. Loop, 5.8–7.9: a blue marker travels once round the loop in 2.0 s, and the dashed "wrong" segment reveals at 6.0. Checks, 23.6–25.9: each tick pulses once per beat, and the blue joint ring draws a 0.5-s highlight at 24.0. AMOEBA, 30.9–33.0: the connectors draw left to right, one per beat, at 31.0, 31.5 and 32.0. Honesty, 43.7–45.4: each numeral gets a one-beat scale pop (1.0, 1.08, 1.0 over 0.5 s) at 44.0, 44.5 and 45.0.

4. **0.0–4.0, the hook does not deliver the script.** The branch floats 90 px above the pipe; there is no plate, no meeting, no gap and no flash. Why it hurts: the only event is a 0.3-s arc, so the viewer watches a static object for three seconds. Fix: 0.0–0.5, the pipe slides 60 px in from the right (ease-out), so the first frame is not empty. 0.5–1.0, the branch lowers 90 px onto the pipe (ease-in-out). 1.8–2.2, a 14-px gap opens on the far side of the seam (ease-out, 0.4 s), and the seam flashes once (+30% brightness for 0.15 s). The gap closes at 3.6 s and hands over to the question.

5. **10.0–12.0, the real application proves nothing.** It is cropped through the DXF button and an "N" badge (11.0), shows no typing, and no flat pattern is in view; it lasts two seconds. Why it hurts: "numbers typed, the pattern appears" is the film's only proof that the product works. Fix: recapture the real application. A real value is typed into the header field, the canvas renders the flat pattern, the crop follows panel borders, the REVIEW REQUIRED banner stays in frame, and the pattern holds for at least 2.0 s. Product screens may be cropped and moved only; no simulated typing.

6. **Phone type.** Card rows, node labels, spec lines, chips and all captions are roughly 22–36 px at 1080p, which is 4–7 px at 390 px wide. Why it hurts: H is 4/10, and the honesty captions carry the promise. Fix: minimum text 40 px at 1920×1080; no card with more than four rows; spec lines move into one 44-px line under the headline; one honesty footer at 40 px in every product scene from 4 s to 50 s; remove the hook caption.

7. **50–54, the end-card disclaimer is not the locked text.** It reads "Development build. Not validated for manufacturing." The script's Fixed rules require "Product concept. Capabilities in development. Not validated for manufacturing." Fix: use the locked line, or have the lead re-lock it before the picture is re-cut. Add a destination (URL or QR) under the pill for 2.5 s; a demo request with no route is not a call to action on a phone.

8. **The disclaimer wording changes in almost every scene** ("not a product view", "data from the product", "Interface simplified…", "Actual software · development build", "Visualisation made for this film…"). Why it hurts: an honesty film cannot run a caption system that contradicts itself. Fix: one caption, one position, one size, one approved sentence (owner to approve the wording), reused in every scene.

9. **35.0–36.0, the colour legend is inverted against the product.** The product's own legend (38.0, full-size) says amber marks what a node depends on and blue marks what a change reaches. The film makes the changed value blue and the flagged downstream nodes amber. Fix: follow the product legend (downstream flags blue, upstream amber), or have the owner decide which is authoritative and make both agree. Make the ripple literal: after the 158 change, the edges draw outward from the changed node at one edge per beat, and each landing node flips on arrival.

10. **Empty frames at 40.0–41.5 and 48.5–50.0.** At 40.0 a numeral stands alone on a blank frame. Fix: the counter at 320 px tall, centred, with the record list below it. At 48.5–50.0 the offline glyph is a corner icon. Fix: the glyph at 600 px, with the blue slash drawn across it over 0.5 s at 48.5. The 28.0 frame is covered by defect 1.

11. **Japanese, must-fix items.** (a) 21–22 s: one language per row; translate every row, or show every row as a product identifier. (b) 53.5: add the equivalent credit to the English film, or remove it from the Japanese one (owner's call). (c) 1.0 s: bring the Japanese headline to the same visual weight as the Latin headline, which it currently exceeds. (d) 4.5–7.9: apply defect 3's beat action.

12. **Headline anchor.** Fix: one headline zone (top-left, 120-px margin) for every scene after the hook, with the object to the right and the card below. Apply it to 8–12, 12–20, 20–26, 30–46 and 50–54.

13. **Object rendering (0.0–4.0, 12–20, 20–26).** The tee and the parts are flat vector gradients. Fix: one lighting rig applied to every 3D part: a key light from the upper left with falloff across each cylinder, a soft contact shadow under each joint, and a 2–3 px rim highlight, so the object reads as steel and matches from scene to scene.

## Nice to have

- Brand mark: "A NEXUS FABRIC" reads as "a Nexus Fabric". Separate the mark with a 24-px gap, or drop it from the top-left lockup.
- 29.5–29.6: a four-frame brightness blip, probably the stamp settling. Make it one clean ease or remove it.
- More than eight disclaimers in one film. Keep the footer, the NOT RELEASED stamp and the 42–46 scene; cut the rest.
- "43 records checked, 0 failing" sits next to the never-said "zero errors". Confirm with the owner, or show the count without "0 failing".
- The AMOEBA end line fades in late. Make it 40 px with a 0.5-s fade that completes on the bar at 53.0.
- The JA terms are unreviewed, as the script says. Include 各種の代 in that review.

## Three moments that must not be lost

1. 33–36, "Change one number.": 150 → 158 mm in blue, the flags, the climbing count. The film's most original idea. Give it 1.5 s more and let the flags travel.
2. 20–26, the checks card: the count climbs to 28 of 28 with the product's identifiers, and "not an approval" stays on screen. The best-timed motion in the film.
3. 42–46, "What it can't confirm, it shows.": REVIEW REQUIRED and the three counts. The film's signature line.

## Verdict

No. Corrections to the present design take it to about 7: the DXF content, the collisions, the holds, the phone type, the JA rows and the disclaimers are all fixable inside the locked boundaries. A 9 needs a different treatment of the picture layer, because the film is a sequence of text-and-card slides with one moving element each. Keep the script, the boundaries, the honesty lines and the three moments above, and change the picture concept:

1. One hero object for the whole film: a steel header with a branch, one key light, and a slow 8° orbit that never leaves frame.
2. Each product claim is an action on that object: the cut curve draws on the joint (0–4 s), the joint unrolls into its flat pattern (8–12 s), the checks light the seams one by one (20–26 s), and the DXF is the unrolled pattern with the stamp across it (26–30 s).
3. AMOEBA is a 2-D graph at the object's width: a changed number pulses the object first, then the flags travel along the product's edges, one per beat (30–42 s).
4. Honesty is one fixed footer at 40 px (NOT RELEASED / DEVELOPMENT BUILD) in every scene from 4 to 50 s, and "What it can't confirm, it shows." is the only line that states it in words.
5. Three type sizes only (headline 150 px, support 54 px, label 40 px), cuts on bar lines with one text zone per frame, and the two real-software captures held full-width for 2.5 s each.
